SNAP Reddit Hyperlink Network · Jan 2014 – Apr 2017

When Attacks Get Answered

858,488 hyperlinks between subreddits · 41,170 directed pairs with at least one hostile link · 8,556 pairs where we can check for a reply
Proximity decides whether hostility gets answered — but only once the two communities actually interact a lot.

At low contact, socially close and socially distant pairs hit back at similar, low rates. At high contact the gap opens fast: distant pairs reciprocate hostility 75% of the time, close pairs only 58%. The effect survives removing hub communities and a 2,000-draw permutation test (p = 0.0005).

How we check if an attack gets answered

  • Counthow many links B sent back to A (n), and how many of those were hostile (k)
  • BaselineB's own overall share of hostile outgoing links, across every subreddit B talks to — not one global rate
  • Chanceprobability B sends back ≥1 hostile link anyway: 1 − (1 − baseline)^n
  • Decidereciprocated if k ≥ 1; compare how often that happens against what chance predicts

Hostility does get answered a little more than chance predicts — but once we remove subreddits that mainly comment on others instead of fighting as equals, the gap shrinks and is no longer reliably different from chance in any group (z = 1.4, 1.8, 0.8). Raw reciprocation is not a settled result on its own.

Does being close change whether it gets answered?

The contact × closeness interaction from a logistic regression of "got a hostile reply back" — negative means closeness matters more as contact increases. Confirmed with a permutation test: shuffling closeness across pairs reproduces an effect this strong in only 1 of 2,000 tries (p = 0.0005).

Hostility clusters into enemy groups

Closed triangles (3 communities, all enemies of each other) — 2.2× more than a randomly rewired network with the same number of enemies per community would produce (p < 0.005). Excluding hub communities, the ratio grows to 3.1×.
Rivalries that are part of a clique happen between more socially distant communities than one-off rivalries — the opposite of what we expected, and it holds after removing hub communities (0.65 vs. 0.41).

Hostile link — SNAP's own sentiment label on the hyperlink (link_sentiment = −1). Hub/meta community — a subreddit with ≥100 distinct targets and a sent/received ratio ≥5 (mostly comments on others rather than fighting as an equal; same rule used across this project).
